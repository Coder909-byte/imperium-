## Composition and assets

The owner’s follow-up approved the imagery and login composition, then requested that the landing itself centre the artwork. A full-width Cole painting now extends behind navigation and the large inscription-style IMPERIUM masthead. The architecture/harbour occupy the open centre; compact copy and actions sit near its lower edge. A light sky treatment protects the dark title, while the lower page uses the painting’s charcoal/green shadows instead of broad cream panels. The mobile hero has its own sharp temple crop and stacked actions. Account/profile pages retain their approved parchment composition; account pages use a compact military-art opening above the form. The atlas → story → quiz explanation explicitly labels the atlas and Gallia available and quizzes/progress planned. Atlas and scenes remain public.

Existing approved Gallia art was inspected but its scoped AI permissions were not reused. The unused Alma-Tadema forge sample was evaluated and temporarily labelled during layout; the owner asked for military/city alternatives, then explicitly chose **Cole for landing and Royer for account screens**. The Alma-Tadema file remains unused by the shell.

- Landing: Thomas Cole, *The Course of Empire: The Consummation of Empire*, 1836, New-York Historical Society. [Specific Commons reproduction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cole_Thomas_The_Consummation_The_Course_of_the_Empire_1836.jpg), current original 7784×5277. Its licensing section explicitly identifies the photographic reproduction as public domain, beyond simply giving the artist’s death date; it also records PD-Art jurisdictional limits. This is an imagined classical city, not a reconstruction of a specific Roman city.
- Account: Lionel Royer, *Vercingetorix before Caesar*, 1899, Musée Crozatier. [Specific Commons reproduction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lionel_Royer_-_Vercingetorix_Throwing_down_His_Weapons_at_the_feet_of_Julius_Caesar.jpg), current original 2204×1469. Its permission section makes the specific faithful-reproduction public-domain statement and notes jurisdictional limits. A later artistic interpretation, not documentary equipment/architectural evidence; no change to Gallia’s scene art.
- Font: Natanael Gama / The Cinzel Project Authors, unmodified Cinzel variable 400–900, from the [official Google Fonts directory](https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/cinzel). The [specific OFL 1.1](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/cinzel/OFL.txt) permits software embedding and redistribution with copyright/licence retained. The font and OFL.txt are local beside the shell components; `next/font/local` loads it with swap and fallback metrics. Font software is OFL, not falsely labelled PD/CC0 artwork. Body text uses system Georgia; ordinary readable capitals, no substituted letters.

Forge produced full-colour 1920×1301 Cole WebP (285,262 bytes) and 1600×1066 Royer WebP (309,290 bytes), with no matte, invented additions or generated restoration. Both entered the manifest at creation; approval, exact reproduction/source and output hashes were then recorded. Next Image supplies intrinsic metadata/blur placeholder plus reserved image containers and responsive `sizes`; only the appropriate painting appears in each rendered page. No asset approval remains pending.
