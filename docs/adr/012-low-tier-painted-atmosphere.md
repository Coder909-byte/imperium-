# 012. Keep painted terrain in the low-tier composition

Gallia's four essential slots were sky, hillfort, inner wall and outer wall. That selection excluded approved painted ground on every low-tier beat, so actors appeared against the sky. Dropping either wall would instead erase the story's double siege. The existing sky and terrain are registered atmospheric cuts and share visibility in all six beats.

Content may explicitly group real, white-tinted, unblurred atmospheric planes with `lowTierGroup`. Validation rejects structures, placeholders and differing per-beat visibility in a group. On initial low tier or a runtime downgrade, compose those already approved images once into a scene-owned canvas texture at 1440px width, in source depth order. The resulting base uses the farthest constituent's depth and essential status if any constituent is essential. It occupies one of the unchanged four rendered plane slots. High tier retains independent parallax planes. No historical art, extra shipped asset or source approval is introduced by this renderer operation.

Low tier sacrifices relative sky/terrain parallax and places the ground behind the retained structural silhouettes. Both original content references and their payload accounting remain intact. This tradeoff requires a composition review against final hill and wall cuts; it does not make those missing structures finished. The temporary composite is destroyed with its owning plane, and decoded source textures remain scene-owned. Per-beat visibility still controls the inner wall from beat 2 and outer wall from beat 3. Debug labels and bounds are explicit scene-lab options, while the public player keeps its artwork-in-progress notice.
